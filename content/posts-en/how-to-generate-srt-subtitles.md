## What is an .SRT Subtitle File?

An **.SRT (SubRip Subtitle)** file is the universal plain-text file format used across all major video editing suites and streaming platforms. It contains:

1. **A sequential caption number** (e.g., `1`, `2`, `3`)
2. **Start and End Timecodes** accurate to milliseconds (`00:00:01,250 --> 00:00:04,500`)
3. **The exact spoken dialogue** on corresponding lines

Because it is lightweight and universally compatible, you can edit formatting, fonts, and animation presets across your favorite video tools without rendering burned-in text beforehand.

## How to Import .SRT into CapCut (Mobile & Desktop)

CapCut is the top mobile and desktop editor for short-form viral content. Here is how to load your `.SRT` file:

### On Desktop:
1. Open your project in CapCut.
2. In the top-left toolbar, click **Text > Auto captions / Local captions**.
3. Select **Import Subtitle** and browse for your downloaded `.srt` file.
4. CapCut will automatically lay down the captions on your timeline, perfectly aligned to your speech.
5. Highlight all captions and apply popular animated styles, stroke outlines, and dynamic brand colors.

---

## How to Import .SRT into Adobe Premiere Pro

1. Launch Premiere Pro and load your active timeline sequence.
2. Go to **File > Import** (or drag the `.srt` file into your Project panel).
3. Drag the `.srt` asset directly onto an empty track above your video in the Timeline.
4. Premiere Pro will open the **Essential Graphics & Captions** panel, allowing you to customize font family, size, background tracking, and safe margin zones across all dialogue simultaneously.

## Pro Tips for High-Retention Subtitles

- **Keep It Short:** Limit captions to 1–2 lines per screen (maximum 4 to 7 words) so viewers can absorb sentences quickly.
- **High-Contrast Colors:** Use vibrant yellow, cyan, or crisp white text over a subtle black drop shadow to ensure readability on any video background.
- **Highlight Trigger Words:** Use CapCut or Premiere's text animator to highlight keywords like *"Mistake"*, *"Free"*, or *"Secret"* in contrasting colors.
