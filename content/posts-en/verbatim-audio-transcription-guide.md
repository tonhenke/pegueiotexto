## What is Verbatim Audio Transcription?

> **Direct Definition:** **Verbatim audio transcription** (often referred to as *true verbatim* or *degravação*) is the technical procedure of capturing and transcribing spoken speech from an audio or audiovisual recording into written text with absolute literal fidelity (*ipsis litteris*). It records every spoken word, stutter, hesitation, grammatical nuance, and background interruption exactly as uttered.

Unlike edited transcripts that prioritize smooth reading, verbatim transcription serves as an exact legal and forensic record. It ensures that no party can dispute what was actually stated or the manner in which it was voiced.

### Indispensable Elements of Verbatim Transcription
A professional verbatim transcript requires:
1. **Accurate Timestamps**: Timecodes (e.g., `[00:04:12]`) mapping statements to the exact second in the original media.
2. **Speaker Diarization**: Unambiguous speaker identification (e.g., *Speaker 1*, *Attorney Smith*, *Witness Doe*).
3. **Phonetic & Grammatical Fidelity**: Preserving slang, colloquialisms, and grammatical choices without editorial sanitization.
4. **Paralinguistic & Environmental Annotations**: Noting long pauses, laughter, emotional interruptions, or unintelligible sections marked as `[inaudible - 00:02:15]`.

## Verbatim vs. Clean Transcription: Key Differences

| Feature | True Verbatim Transcription (*Ipsis Litteris*) | Clean Verbatim Transcription | Edited / Summary Transcription |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fidelity to Spoken Words** | **100% exact & literal** | High fidelity to meaning, polished for readability | Focuses on key takeaways and highlights |
| **Grammatical Errors** | **Preserved exactly** as spoken | Lightly corrected when unobtrusive | Fully adapted to standard written prose |
| **Fillers (*"um"*, *"you know"*, stutters)** | **Retained in full** | **Removed** for clean flow | **Removed** |
| **Pauses & Hesitations** | **Logged with timestamps** | **Omitted** | **Omitted** |
| **Primary Use Cases** | **Court trials, forensic probes, depositions, police inquiries** | **Corporate meetings, podcasts, lectures, video subtitles** | **Executive summaries, news briefs, internal memos** |
